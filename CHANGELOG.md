## Unreleased

## 0.8.17 - 2026-11-16

- A TASK'S WORD IS WORKED OUT FROM ITS RUNS, NEVER SET. A task is stored open, done or dropped; a run is one worker's go at it (an agent by the engine's id, or the session itself), with its job, PC, repo, branch, start commit, state note, last heard, how it ended and its report. One function on the board gives every task its word from its runs, edges, gates and wakes, and every surface reads it: running while a run is heard from, unconfirmed once a run has gone quiet for three minutes (a task whose session's process ended reads so until that session is reopened or the user takes it over), waiting with what holds it (`after T-577`, `after Q617`), else ready to start, ready to continue (the last run handed on, failed or was taken over) or ready to settle (its agent finished: "ready to settle: home-nodes finished, 2 steps open"). The stored statuses in progress, blocked and deferred, the agent tags and the landing and stale words are gone, and `show`, the lists, the picture, Home, the track card, the tray console and the board's pages say the one word with who and since. A step keeps the number it was given for good, with its own order, state and a word on a done one, and every way of closing a task goes through one gate that refuses while a step is open, naming each step and the calls that settle it. A close over another session's open run needs the user's take-over. Board migration 015 turns each task's old status into runs and numbers today's steps; the export and import carry runs.
- THE KICKOFF IS AN INDEX, AND A RESUME GIVES NOTHING BACK IN HAND. The kickoff names the track, its goal (and what it was, when the last handoff restated it), its chain and focus, then its tasks by what they need: unconfirmed, to settle, to continue, ready to start and running, with counts and how long each has been ready, then the critical path, what waits on the user, answers since, the newest ten track notes with the command that reads the rest, the decisions made or amended this leg and the handoff's state. It carries no step texts and no note fragments. A handoff ends the session's runs as handed on, so the taken track's tasks read ready to continue and the new session takes what it judges. A track taken with `/askerdo resume` works under its own rules from the kickoff turn's first step, and every turn the mod starts carries the changed rule blocks.
- A SESSION FINISHES WHAT IT STARTED, AND THE USER'S HOLD WINS. When the main loop is about to stop, the mod reads the board: with workers in flight, the stop is refused only when a run the session started has ended and its task is not settled, naming the task, its open steps and the call that settles it; with nothing in flight, a stop with ready work left is refused once, naming it, unless the session handed off, ran out of ready tasks or waits on the user. A refusal fires once per stop, so no loop is possible. A hold the user gives is recorded on the track with their words: the track reads "on hold, your word" on Home, the track card and the band, and no stop is refused until it is lifted. An agent's report arrives with its task, its open steps by number and the settling call, read from the board at that moment.
- AN AGENT'S RUN COMES FROM THE ENGINE'S OWN WORD. The mod opens an agent's run when the engine answers the dispatch, on the tasks the prompt names (else the session's own open run of that job), at the agent's own worktree, branch and start commit; one timer a minute renews the lease and every live agent's run in one write; the run ends when the engine says the agent ended, with its report. The join lives on the board, so a mod reload, a `/clear` or a new process loses no run, and a resumed agent opens its run where its last one worked. An agent that has handed its report back ends at its turn even with background work of its own still running; one that waits on its background work keeps its run, and one that ends its turn with neither is refused once and told to hand back. A run past the 30 minute bound with no word reads handed on, and the agent's later report lands on its tasks as a note. A spawn onto another session's run is refused, and a commit or dispatch beside the session's own agent run on a task counts as on-task work.
- AGENTS REPORT, AND THE SESSION WRITES THE BOARD. An agent's own task writes, its `place`, `fact` and `decide` calls and its shell `knowledge new`, `amend`, `edit`, `supersede` and `decide` are refused with an answer telling it to put the finding in its report; the agent write guard counts a write quoted inside a shell wrapper or behind env options, and lets a call naming a scratch board or asking for help through. A dispatch hands the worker the rules in force and the task block, and the worker reports on each step by its number for the session to tick (`task_update` takes a word on a done step).
- WORK FOR SOMEONE ELSE MOVES OUT OF A TASK IN THE CALL THAT FINDS IT. A step its worker will not do moves with `task_update` `moveSteps` (`--move-steps`): onto an open task, into a new task by the found work ladder, or to an open question, each struck with where it went. The user's look is a task labelled `look` that the session runs: its last act is one question listing what needs the user's eye, which ends the session's run on the task, and "not yet" leaves it ready with the user's words in its block. Work only a later session can do waits on its track with `wake next-session`, ready in the first kickoff of another session; the inbox refuses that wake. An outcome of several jobs is a closing task that waits by edges on its parts: `track promote` and `track fold` are gone, and board migration 016 makes each open promoted track's pointer such a closing task. The look close, the landed mark and the close on a fact are gone; a look or a fact close is refused with its way out.
- WORK A SESSION FINDS IS PLACED AT A REAL PLACE, AND ANYWHERE ELSE ASKS THE USER FIRST. Found work goes on the track the session holds at once only at a real place in its order, naming what must not finish without it (`before`). Placing it last on that track, or off it (another track, a parked track that opens on an event, another workstream, the inbox, a later session), creates nothing: the board registers a placement question that shows the session's reason, the place asked for and the real place on its own track, with four presses: do it on this track, put it where asked, let's talk where it fits, or drop it. The press creates the task at the place pressed, as the filing session, with no model turn, and a track does not finish while such a question of its work is open. Only the user's own act answers one: a session's answer or drop, a shell filing that claims the user or another session, and a bare number that is not the question's id are refused. The `place` tool files by tier, a close's or a handoff's leftovers take the same places, a filing of new work needs a description, a note naming something left answers with the calls that give it a home, and the `fact` tool records a project fact as a knowledge record. The handoff, the kickoff and the Tracks tab's Found this arc row list what an arc found and where each went, the unplaced first. On the CLI, `task create`, `task edit`, `track capture` and leftovers take `--before`, `--after`, `--last` and `--why-elsewhere`, and every refusal offers only a call the board takes.
- A DECISION STATES ONLY WHAT THE USER CHOSE, AND IS CONFIRMED BY THEIR PRESS. `decide` puts the user's choice in the title, the session's own picks in the body and the user's words whole, and warns when the words end in a question or a hedge. A ruling is confirmed when the question it names is answered with the option its title states, or with `--confirmed`; the task block, briefs and notes then carry its title and K-n, and `show K-n` says whether it is confirmed. `decide` with no files or commands governs the commands the turn ran and no file unless named; its proposed commands leave out the session's git bookkeeping. A decision's body and amends reach the pointer, the brief and the task block, and an amend tells the holders.
- DECISIONS AND KNOWLEDGE RECORDS POINT ON READS AND SEARCHES TOO. A glob marked `(read)`, `(search)` or `(edit read)` points a session at its record when it reads a matching file or runs a search whose folder can hold one (a `| text` part wants the search's pattern to hold it), once per record per session. A call that matches several records draws one counted toast, and past three one block of titles instead of each ruling in full. A command fragment matches a run file only at a path boundary and a slash command's never a path argument, and an edit's contains word matches in any case.
- A HANDOFF WAITS FOR THE SESSION'S WORK TO LAND, AND KEEPS WHAT IT IS TOLD TO KEEP. While an agent or workflow the session started still runs, `handoff_write`, `/askerdo handoff`, `park` and a shell `handoff write` store nothing and give no resume block; the answer names what runs with its running time, the track stays with the session, and one note comes when the last has landed. An agent that stops to wait on background work of its own counts as running. A kept `branches` group keeps every branch, only a kept `merged` group or the worktree's own path keeps a merged, clean worktree's folder, the result names each kept one with its reason, and a write removes only agent worktrees and never a stub branch whose worktree still stands. A handoff restates the track's goal, the old text kept in its history, and a track born with no goal gets its first one there; it takes each task's note in the same call, carries its waits into the next until one drops them, wraps a state line over 400 characters instead of refusing it, and names its unpushed commits. Its verify entries carry each repo's remote and resolve on the PC that reads them, and it can name the PC its next leg runs on.
- THE SWEEP FINDS WHAT EARLIER SESSIONS LEFT BEHIND. `/askerdo:sweep`, and `askerdo sweep`, digest a track's sessions or the project's since the last sweep from this PC's transcripts and scan the board; one reader agent checks each item against the board and the repo, and each lost or stale one gets the nearest home that comes back on its own, under the placement rule. `track finish` and `track drop` offer it, and the built-in nothing-left-behind rule line asks for it in the session's words.
- THE TASK BLOCK CARRIES THE JOB TO EVERY WORKER. `show T-n` prints a task's block by default (intent, description, steps by number, answered gates, decisions in force, notes since the last rewrite, its word and its last run), `task block` reads several ids and names each one's track, an agent dispatch carries the block of each task it serves after the rules, and coverage and a review's block carry each covered task's intent and decisions. A note can rewrite a task's title, description and intent, or a track's goal, the old text kept with the note as why. Rule lines take `--for agents:<role>`, a subagent type or a task label, and ride only that agent's brief. A dispatch's tasks are the open tasks its job, ids or opening sentence name, and one naming tasks the mod cannot read is told so.
- A DONE REVIEW STAYS DONE, AND A COMMIT LINKS ONLY THE TASKS ITS SUBJECT LEADS WITH. A commit after a completed review counts as unreviewed work for the next review instead of reopening the done one, and the finish lock at the full review depth holds for it too. A commit links the task ids its subject line leads with (`T-13: ...`); an id cited later links nothing, so citing a reviewed task never reopens its reviews. A review placed on a track with no `before` still lands before the track's closing review.
- PROCEDURE CARDS MATCH WHAT A COMMAND RUNS, AND A DEVIATED RUN NAMES ITS OUTCOME. A card's file trigger matches only a command that runs the file, never a `git add`, commit or copy of it, and a flag or env trigger matches the command it rides. `proc ran <name> deviated` takes `--fixed <commit>` (refused for a commit no later than the card's verified one), `--one-off "why"` or `--filed T-n`, and a one-off at a step that was a one-off last time is refused. Only a card that opts in with `record: command` is recorded as a clean run by itself; every other card's pointer asks the session to record its run. A task names its cards with their badges in `show`, its page and the kickoff.
- THE DESKTOP BAND IS ONE NEEDS LINE OVER THE STATUS LINE. In every state and at every width the band draws a lead and up to three chips fitted by one plan from the pane's columns, the answer strip and its note field on that line, queued answers on a chip whose card names each, and any dropped chip named on the mark's card; the queued chip keeps its count and Send at 238 px, the question giving up its name, mark and letters first. The band holds questions and what blocks them only: the calls only the user can close (a stalled track, a review due, an override) are pressed on Home and the board's Needs you page, an override also on its card, and a problem with AskerDo on this PC is the status line's amber Health chip, whose press runs, copies or opens the fix and which reads update when a refused protocol's fix is on this PC. The Agents chip counts agents and background tasks, and its card names each kind and lists the newest eight finished agents; the Task chip names the task the session has in hand. The offline row is one line cut at the band's width with every word and the fix in its hover card, runs one status check when it first draws, and clears at the first online answer. An updates waiting row names what waits, who sent it and from which PC.
- EVERY DRAW IN THE MOD COMES FROM ONE STORE AND ONE SCHEDULER. Every state write goes through one store of versioned slices: a press draws at once, a passive cause at most once a second, and a Send as one batch. The band, the status row, each pane tab and each question card draw from models built once per version of the slices they read, the checks compare models and build no tree, and a settled card keeps its last drawing. The session's own slices sit in one snapshot key, so a respawned hooks worker draws the tab, open strips, typed notes and the answer queue at once and finishes a Send. An answer or a Send draws each surface twice, at the press and once its write, reload and turn have landed: in the kit an answer on the band went from 4 asks of the band and pane to 2, and a Send of two from 3 to 2. A Home picture compared by content is reused when a board load leaves it unchanged.
- THE DESKTOP DRAWINGS STAY UNDER THE PAGE'S BOUNDS. Every drawing is counted the way the desktop page counts it, and past the ceiling each surface gives way in a fixed order, saying where it shows what it left out: Home its oldest rows, the Tracks tab each section's tracks past a count, the Questions tab its list past a count, then the open card's long words, the band its questions from elsewhere, then its running agent rows, then the waiting card's items, and a reply's question cards past a count fold into one line, while every press stays. Each ladder places its cut from two built counts, so a draw past the ceiling builds 2 to 5 times where a search built 16 to 21; a band of 150 running agents and 300 questions from elsewhere went from 3567 nodes to 484. The band's ceiling takes the other plugins' rows off. A long track's bar costs a quarter of the characters with every pixel as before (a 316-task bar 6,145 characters against 23,132), a bar past 1500 tasks draws by pixel column, and an Activity row draws in 25 page nodes instead of 30, so a busy Home on a 322-task track keeps every row.
- HOME SHOWS EVERY AGENT, SHELL, MONITOR AND WORKFLOW IN FLIGHT, WITH A STOP ON EACH. Under Agents a row per agent carries its depth meter, job, model, running time, tool calls, current tool, depth and the tasks it serves, a helper indented under the agent that started it; a workflow is one row with its agents under it and its one Stop. Background lists shells and monitors, each led by its kind's icon, and a main loop shell command still running after 2 s gets a "foreground command" row until it returns. Finished agents and background tasks wait in done folds under the running ones. A background command leaves the running rows when its end reaches the session, mid-turn or from an agent's own conversation (34 running rows had shown while 18 shells lived). A row with no description is named from the first clause of its command that does work, and anything named as a token, key, secret, password, credential or authorization, and a URL's user and password, draws as `***`. Every running background task and background agent has a 20 by 20 px stop square in one column at the right: the first press arms it, the second stops it as TaskStop does, and a refused stop says why.
- A RUNNING AGENT'S ROW HOLDS ITS SIZE AND ITS PARTS FROM TICK TO TICK. On Home and in the band's agents card a running row takes a fixed size worked out from its detail at its widest (measured over five ticks at every card width from 8 to 42 cells, 39 of 70 cases moved before and none after), keeps its model, time, calls and depth in place with the tool in use last and cut to the room left, and gives up the depth's limit first, past an hour and past 99 calls too. Every running line takes its answer on the mod's one wall second on its own tick and asks at most once in 900 ms, so the app never counts a render loop against a line another line updated, and the card's rows fit the lifted card's room, up to 360 px.
- THE TRACKS PICTURE DRAWS FANS, SIDE LINES AND FOLDS AT EVERY WIDTH. A wide fan draws as one bus with ticks and a caption, a fan into a trunk station sits in a group box, and side work rides tunnels whose portals press their twin; a folded group of side work counts its tasks by word and wears its own mark, named in the legend. Every rail mark, a station's or a pill's train included, presses what it serves, on the board page and in the pane. A fold row's portal or the "+N more on the board" link that does not fit takes its own line, a top chip takes the room beside it before cutting a name, an edge chip's track name takes its chip's room, a critical path tie goes to the lower task number, the wait chip and the track page's waiting tile count only waiting tasks, and a held finish's padlock stays whole inside the bar. - THE DOCTOR CHECKS THE SHAPE OF A PROJECT'S TASKS. It finds open steps a task's worker may not do (naming the user, a look, real use or a later session), old look closes and closes on a fact, tasks once blocked or deferred on nothing named, tasks with no done clause or their job moved into their notes, runs unconfirmed for over a day and inbox tasks with no wake. - THE CONTRACT TEACHES THE TASK SHAPE AND THE PLACEMENT RULE. The output style says a task's word is computed and never set, the found work ladder with moved steps and next-session, a look's one question, the handoff's ended runs and the kickoff index, the stop refusals and the hold, the placement rule, the carried statement of a confirmed ruling and a goal that follows the track. - THE BOARD SPEAKS PROTOCOL 4: UPDATE THE BOARD AND EVERY PC IN ONE SITTING. Runs, the computed word and the placement question change what a PC sends and what the board accepts, so the board refuses a PC on another protocol and that PC's status row names the side to update. ## 0.8.16 - 2026-11-08

- ONBOARDING CONVERTS A PROJECT'S OLD WORKFLOW INTO ASKERDO. `/askerdo:onboard` moves the system a project worked by onto the board: rules sessions still load become rule lines, work in flight a track with its handoff and tasks, rulings decisions, open questions questions, facts knowledge records and routines procedure cards; run logs, briefs and finished plans leave with their folder and stay in git. Claude writes the plan from a survey's facts and settles what the repo or the board answers; the rest reaches the user as band questions. A rehearsal on a scratch board and a copy of the repo, read by a fresh agent, shows what will land, and one go covers the board write and every repo edit. The review page is gone. Measured on a large game project: 464 board changes, 0 refused, 0 coverage gaps over 75 sources, CLAUDE.md from 6,759 to 1,224 bytes, and an undo that gave back the exact tree.
- /ASKERDO:DOCTOR CHECKS A PROJECT ALREADY ON THE BOARD. A local server that does not answer its ping in time reads as busy, with the fix to check again or run `askerdo board --restart`, and `askerdo board` words that refusal the same way and never stops a server it could not identify. It runs in the asking process, so it judges that copy rather than the local server's. The troubleshooting page starts from it.
- THE BAND AND THE STATUS ROW SHOW THIS PC'S FIRST PROBLEM WITH ITS FIX. They draw the first problem `askerdo status` finds: Start or Restart runs the local server's fix in place, Copy puts the join-code command for another PC on the clipboard, and a healthy PC draws nothing. The mod looks again when a call finds the local server gone, after a fix, each minute while a problem stands and at once when `board/local.json` changes under it, running `askerdo status` once per new problem.
- A SESSION RESUMED ACROSS AN UPDATE IS TOLD WHAT TO DO. A tool call with a field name the installed AskerDo does not take is refused with the installed version and the fix to start a new session, in the mod's handoff tools, the CLI's handoff write and update and the board's handoff update. The board's refusal of a handoff key an AskerDo older than 0.8.13 sends says to update that PC with `claude plugin update askerdo@askerdo` and start a new session.
- THE NEEDS YOU FOOTER DRAWS UNDER THE QUESTION CARDS. A reply that asks pastes the question's block in its footer, under the footer's label. On the desktop the mod draws the footer under the last question card, so it is the last thing on screen however many cards the reply asks, and folds each block in it to one line naming the question and saying it is on the card above, so the reply points at the card once. The contract, the ask tool's result and description, the CLI's register result and the question file doc say so, and the golden fixtures are regenerated.
- FOUND WORK ALWAYS NAMES ITS PLACE. A task a session files names a track (with `--before` and `--after` for the steps around it) or `--unplaced` with a reason, else the filing is refused naming both ways and the board's hint, the open track sharing the most labels, files and words with it. `track found <TR-n>` lists what a track's arc filed, where each went and the command that moves it, and the handoff, the kickoff, a release's question and the Tracks tab's Found this arc row (the unplaced first) show the same. `task edit` moves a task between tracks (`--track`, `--before`, `--after`, `--unplaced`), and task create, task edit, track capture and track layout name the session from `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` when none is passed. The mod's inbox capture and the join card's field-bug task pass their unplaced reason.
- THE STATION MAP DRAWS EVERY WAIT AS A LINE. The waits into one station share one trunk, the lines out of one station share one trunk, a wait no single gap serves turns twice along the band between two rows, and no bend crosses a station's words. A release waiting on 13 tasks draws all 13 (10 in 0.8.13). Measured on 420 seeded random tracks: drawings with a line through a station's words fall from 153 to 0, and far waits kept as words from 225 to 108.
- THE DISPATCH CHECK FLAGS ONLY A BRIEF THAT ASKS TO PIN WORDING. Its pin wording row flags a brief line asking for a test that pins exact wording, never a line about regenerating golden fixtures.
- THE LOCAL SERVER ANSWERS ONLY ITS OWN HOST NAMES. It takes `askerdo.localhost`, `localhost` and `127.0.0.1` on its port, and refuses a link carrying the older `t-ask-and-ding.localhost` host.
